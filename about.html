Who Spurgeon is

Charles Haddon Spurgeon (19 June 1834 to 31 January 1892) was an English Particular Baptist preacher. He was born in Kelvedon, England, and died in Menton, France. Wikipedia records that some readers still call him the “Prince of Preachers.”

He was pastor of the New Park Street Chapel congregation in London, later known as the Metropolitan Tabernacle, for 38 years. The congregation moved into the purpose-built Tabernacle at Elephant and Castle, Southwark, on 18 March 1861. While there he also supported practical work that included a Pastors’ College (later named Spurgeon’s College) and the Stockwell Orphanage.

Spurgeon wrote sermons, commentaries, and devotionals. Many sermons were taken down as he spoke and printed in his lifetime. This class site uses only the public-domain English text of Morning and Evening.

About the book

Morning and Evening: Daily Readings is arranged by month. It gives one morning meditation and one evening meditation for each day of the year. Each piece opens from a short Scripture text. The Christian Classics Ethereal Library (CCEL) hosts the work and treats the English text as public domain.

Book description follows the CCEL work page for Morning and Evening: Daily Readings (retrieved 2 October 2026).

Who this site is for
