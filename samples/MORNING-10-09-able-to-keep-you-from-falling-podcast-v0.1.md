## Script

[music: short sting, then under]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

**"Able to keep you from falling."**

[pause]

Jude, verse twenty-four. "Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy."

[beat]

Able to keep you from falling.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** The road to heaven is, in one sense, the safest road there is. In another sense, there is no road on earth so dangerous. [beat] It is full of difficulties. One wrong step (and how easily we take it when grace is absent), and down we go.

**(2)** You know the feeling. It has rained all night over London. The steps down into the Underground are slick and shining, and the yellow sign at the top says, quite reasonably, Caution: Slippery Surface. Some of us walk a path like that every day of our lives. How often have we had to say with the psalmist, "My feet were almost gone; my steps had well nigh slipped."

**(3)** If we were sure-footed climbers, the kind who go up a rock face without a second thought, it would not matter so much. [beat] But look at us. On the best roads we falter. On the smoothest paths we stumble. These weak knees of ours can hardly carry our own wobbling weight. A straw could trip us. A pebble could cut us. We are toddlers, really, taking our first unsteady steps in the walk of faith, and our heavenly Father is holding us up by the arms, or we would be flat on the pavement in a moment. [softer] You have seen it on the South Bank on a Saturday afternoon: a tiny child, a parent bent double behind, two small hands lifted high. [pause] That is you. That is me.

**(4)** So if we have been kept from falling, how we ought to bless the patient power that watches over us, day after day! [building] Think how prone we are to sin. Think how quick we are to choose danger, how strong the pull in us to throw ourselves down. Think on these things, and they will make you sing more sweetly than you have ever sung before: "Glory be to him, who is able to keep us from falling."

[pause]

**(5)** And there is more. We have enemies, and they are trying to push us over. The road is rough, and we are weak, and on top of all that there are foes lying in wait. They rush out when we least expect them, and they work hard to trip us up, or to hurl us off the nearest edge. [beat] They are not the people beside you on the platform. They are older and darker than that, and you will not see them coming.

**(6)** [slower] Only an almighty arm can keep us safe from enemies we cannot see. And such an arm is engaged for our defence. [pause] He who promised is faithful. He is able to keep you from falling. [beat] Able.

**(7)** So hold these two things together. A deep sense of your own complete weakness. And a firm belief in your perfect safety. Not because your feet are steady, but because his hand is. Then you can stand at the top of those wet steps this morning and say, with joyful confidence, in the words of the old hymn:

[slower, almost spoken as verse]

"Against me earth and hell combine,
But on my side is power divine;
Jesus is all, and he is mine!"

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Faithful Father, we confess that we are weak, quick to wander and quick to fall. Thank you that our safety does not rest on our footing but on your keeping. Hold us by the arms today, on the wet steps and the crowded platforms, at our desks and on the wards, and guard us from the enemies we cannot see. Keep us from falling, and one day present us faultless before your glory with exceeding joy, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

[pause]

### Sign-off (optional)

Go carefully today, and go confidently. He is able.

[music: up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 9**. Text as given in the CCEL edition (ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/morneve); it matches the seed file `data/readings/2026-10-09-morning.md` word for word. Spelling and punctuation are original. Dash check: this reading contains no dashes, so nothing was altered.

> "Able to keep you from falling."
>
> Jude 24
>
> In some sense the path to heaven is very safe, but in other respects there is no road so dangerous. It is beset with difficulties. One false step (and how easy it is to take that if grace be absent), and down we go. What a slippery path is that which some of us have to tread! How many times have we to exclaim with the Psalmist, "My feet were almost gone, my steps had well nigh slipped." If we were strong, sure-footed mountaineers, this would not matter so much; but in ourselves, how weak we are! In the best roads we soon falter, in the smoothest paths we quickly stumble. These feeble knees of ours can scarcely support our tottering weight. A straw may throw us, and a pebble can wound us; we are mere children tremblingly taking our first steps in the walk of faith, our heavenly Father holds us by the arms or we should soon be down. Oh, if we are kept from falling, how must we bless the patient power which watches over us day by day! Think, how prone we are to sin, how apt to choose danger, how strong our tendency to cast ourselves down, and these reflections will make us sing more sweetly than we have ever done, "Glory be to him, who is able to keep us from falling." We have many foes who try to push us down. The road is rough and we are weak, but in addition to this, enemies lurk in ambush, who rush out when we least expect them, and labour to trip us up, or hurl us down the nearest precipice. Only an Almighty arm can preserve us from these unseen foes, who are seeking to destroy us. Such an arm is engaged for our defence. He is faithful that hath promised, and he is able to keep us from falling, so that with a deep sense of our utter weakness, we may cherish a firm belief in our perfect safety, and say, with joyful confidence,
>
> "Against me earth and hell combine,
> But on my side is power divine;
> Jesus is all, and he is mine!"
