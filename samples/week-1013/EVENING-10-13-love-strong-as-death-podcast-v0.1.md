## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Think of the strongest love you have ever seen in a London hospital corridor, and then picture a love that went through death and won.

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

The Song of Solomon, chapter eight, verse six. "Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm: for love is strong as death; jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame."

[beat]

Love is strong as death.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Whose love can this be, that is as mighty as death itself, the conqueror of kings, the destroyer of the human race? [beat] It would sound like mockery if it were said of my love for Jesus, my poor, weak, barely breathing love. I do love him. Perhaps, by his grace, I could even die for him. But my love on its own can hardly stand a sneering joke at work, let alone a cruel death.

**(2)** No. Surely it is my Beloved's love that is spoken of here: the love of Jesus, the matchless lover of souls. His love really was stronger than the most terrible death, because it went through the trial of the cross and came out triumphant.

**(3)** [building] It was a slow death, but love outlasted the torment. It was a shameful death, but love despised the shame. It was a death under sentence, but love carried our sins. It was a forsaken, lonely death, with the eternal Father hiding his face, but love endured the curse and triumphed over all of it. [pause] Never such love. Never such death. It was a desperate fight, but love took the prize.

**(4)** [softer] So what now, my heart? Do you feel nothing, looking at a love like that? [beat] Yes, Lord, I do. I long, I ache, to feel your love blazing like a furnace inside me. Come yourself, and set my spirit on fire.

[slower, almost spoken as verse]
"For every drop of crimson blood
Thus shed to make me live,
O wherefore, wherefore have not I
A thousand lives to give?"

**(6)** Why should I give up hope of loving Jesus with a love as strong as death? He deserves it. I want it. The martyrs had that love, and they were only flesh and blood; so why not me? They grieved over their weakness, and yet out of weakness they were made strong. [beat] Grace gave them every ounce of their courage, and there is the same grace for me.

**(7)** [slower] Jesus, lover of my soul, pour out that love, your own love, into my heart, this evening.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord Jesus, our love for you is small and easily shaken, but your love went through the cross and was not beaten by death. Thank you for bearing our sins and the curse for us. Tonight, as the day goes quiet, pour your love into our hearts until it burns. Give us the same grace you gave the martyrs, so that out of weakness we are made strong, and love you with a love as strong as death. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

His love was stronger than death. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 13**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Song of Solomon 8:6**
>
> "Love is strong as death."
>
> Whose love can this be which is as mighty as the conqueror of monarchs, the destroyer of the human race? Would it not sound like satire if it were applied to my poor, weak, and scarcely living love to Jesus my Lord? I do love him, and perhaps by his grace, I could even die for him, but as for my love in itself, it can scarcely endure a scoffing jest, much less a cruel death. Surely it is my Beloved's love which is here spoken of--the love of Jesus, the matchless lover of souls. His love was indeed stronger than the most terrible death, for it endured the trial of the cross triumphantly. It was a lingering death, but love survived the torment; a shameful death, but love despised the shame; a penal death, but love bore our iniquities; a forsaken, lonely death, from which the eternal Father hid his face, but love endured the curse, and gloried over all. Never such love, never such death. It was a desperate duel, but love bore the palm. What then, my heart? Hast thou no emotions excited within thee at the contemplation of such heavenly affection? Yes, my Lord, I long, I pant to feel thy love flaming like a furnace within me. Come thou thyself and excite the ardour of my spirit.
>
> "For every drop of crimson blood
>
> Thus shed to make me live,
>
> O wherefore, wherefore have not I
>
> A thousand lives to give?"
>
> Why should I despair of loving Jesus with a love as strong as death? He deserves it: I desire it. The martyrs felt such love, and they were but flesh and blood, then why not I? They mourned their weakness, and yet out of weakness were made strong. Grace gave them all their unflinching constancy--there is the same grace for me. Jesus, lover of my soul, shed abroad such love, even thy love in my heart, this evening.
