## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Oxford Street on a Saturday sells a thousand ways to fit in; tonight the question is what it costs a Christian to blend in.

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Romans, chapter twelve, verse two. "And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God."

[beat]

And be not conformed to this world.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** If a Christian can possibly be saved while living just like the world, then at best it will be "so as by fire". [beat] A salvation that bare is almost as much to be dreaded as desired.

**(2)** Friend, do you want to leave this world in the dark, on a gloomy deathbed, and get into heaven the way a shipwrecked sailor scrambles up the rocks of his home shore? Then be worldly. Mix in with people who live for money, and refuse to go outside the camp, carrying Christ's reproach.

**(3)** [building] But do you want a heaven down here as well as a heaven up there? Do you want to grasp, with all the saints, the height and depth, and to know the love of Christ which passes knowledge? Do you want a rich welcome into the joy of your Lord? Then "come out from among them, and be ye separate, and touch not the unclean thing."

**(4)** Do you want the full assurance of faith? You cannot have it while your closest fellowship is with sinners. Do you want to burn with strong love? Your love will be soaked through by godless company. [pause] You cannot become a great Christian. You may be a baby in grace, but you can never be a grown man in Christ Jesus while you give yourself up to the world's maxims and the world's ways of doing business.

**(5)** It is a bad thing for an heir of heaven to be close friends with the heirs of hell. It looks bad when a courtier is too friendly with his king's enemies. Even small inconsistencies are dangerous. [beat] Little thorns make big blisters. Little moths ruin fine clothes. And little frivolities and little dishonesties will rob your faith of a thousand joys.

**(6)** [slower] You who say you are a Christian, but are too little set apart from sinners: you have no idea what you are losing by fitting in with the world. It cuts the tendons of your strength, and makes you crawl where you ought to run.

**(7)** So, for your own comfort's sake, and for the sake of your growth in grace, if you are a Christian, be a Christian. [pause] And be a marked and distinct one.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord, we confess how easily we fit in with the world, and how often it dampens our love for you. Thank you that Christ bore reproach outside the camp for us. Give us courage to go out to him, to be set apart in the small things as well as the large, at work, at home and online. Do not let us settle for a bare salvation. Make us marked and distinct Christians, full of your joy, for Jesus' sake. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Be a Christian, and a distinct one. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 14**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Romans 12:2**
>
> "And be not conformed to this world."
>
> If a Christian can by possibility be saved while he conforms to this world, at any rate it must be so as by fire. Such a bare salvation is almost as much to be dreaded as desired. Reader, would you wish to leave this world in the darkness of a desponding death bed, and enter heaven as a shipwrecked mariner climbs the rocks of his native country? then be worldly; be mixed up with Mammonites, and refuse to go without the camp bearing Christ's reproach. But would you have a heaven below as well as a heaven above? Would you comprehend with all saints what are the heights and depths, and know the love of Christ which passeth knowledge? Would you receive an abundant entrance into the joy of your Lord? Then come ye out from among them, and be ye separate, and touch not the unclean thing. Would you attain the full assurance of faith? you cannot gain it while you commune with sinners. Would you flame with vehement love? Your love will be damped by the drenchings of godless society. You cannot become a great Christian--you may be a babe in grace, but you never can be a perfect man in Christ Jesus while you yield yourself to the worldly maxims and modes of business of men of the world. It is ill for an heir of heaven to be a great friend with the heirs of hell. It has a bad look when a courtier is too intimate with his king's enemies. Even small inconsistencies are dangerous. Little thorns make great blisters, little moths destroy fine garments, and little frivolities and little rogueries will rob religion of a thousand joys. O professor, too little separated from sinners, you know not what you lose by your conformity to the world. It cuts the tendons of your strength, and makes you creep where you ought to run. Then, for your own comfort's sake, and for the sake of your growth in grace, if you be a Christian, be a Christian, and be a marked and distinct one.
