## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Tonight's verse is about a donkey, a lamb and a broken neck, and stranger still, it turns out to be about you.

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Exodus, chapter thirty-four, verse twenty. "But the firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb: and if thou redeem him not, then shalt thou break his neck. All the firstborn of thy sons thou shalt redeem. And none shall appear before me empty."

[beat]

But the firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb: and if thou redeem him not, then shalt thou break his neck.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Every firstborn creature had to belong to the Lord. But the donkey was an unclean animal, so it could not be offered in sacrifice. [beat] What then? Was it to go free from the universal law? Not at all. God allows no exceptions. The donkey is his by right, but he will not accept it. He will not lower his claim, and yet he cannot be pleased with that victim.

**(2)** There was only one way out: redemption. The creature had to be saved by a lamb standing in its place. If it was not redeemed, it had to die.

**(3)** [slower] My soul, there is a lesson here for you. [pause] That unclean animal is you. You rightly belong to the Lord who made you and keeps you alive. But you are so sinful that God will not, and cannot, accept you. It has come to this: the Lamb of God must stand in your place, or you must die eternally.

**(4)** So let the whole world know how grateful you are to that spotless Lamb, who has already bled for you, and so redeemed you from the deadly curse of the law.

**(5)** Do you think the Israelite ever had to ask himself which should die, the donkey or the lamb? Would a good man not stop to weigh them up? [beat] There is certainly no comparison between the worth of a human soul and the life of the Lord Jesus. And yet the Lamb dies, and man, the donkey, is spared.

**(6)** [building] My soul, wonder at the boundless love of God to you and to the rest of the human race. Worms are bought with the blood of the Son of the Most High! Dust and ashes, redeemed at a price far above silver and gold! What a fate would have been mine, if plentiful redemption had not been found!

**(7)** [slower] Breaking the donkey's neck was only a moment's penalty. But who can measure the wrath to come, which has no limit we can imagine? [pause] Beyond all price is the glorious Lamb who has redeemed us from a fate like that.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lamb of God, we were unclean and could not be accepted, and you stood in our place. Thank you for bleeding for us and redeeming us from the curse of the law. Tonight we wonder again that worms and dust should be bought with your blood. Let our gratitude show in how we live, so that everyone can see it. Keep us resting in your redemption, now and at the last. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

You were bought by the Lamb. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 15**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Exodus 34:20**
>
> "But the firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb: and if thou redeem him not, then shalt thou break his neck."
>
> Every firstborn creature must be the Lord's, but since the ass was unclean, it could not be presented in sacrifice. What then? Should it be allowed to go free from the universal law? By no means. God admits of no exceptions. The ass is his due, but he will not accept it; he will not abate the claim, but yet he cannot be pleased with the victim. No way of escape remained but redemption--the creature must be saved by the substitution of a lamb in its place; or if not redeemed, it must die. My soul, here is a lesson for thee. That unclean animal is thyself; thou art justly the property of the Lord who made thee and preserves thee, but thou art so sinful that God will not, cannot, accept thee; and it has come to this, the Lamb of God must stand in thy stead, or thou must die eternally. Let all the world know of thy gratitude to that spotless Lamb who has already bled for thee, and so redeemed thee from the fatal curse of the law. Must it not sometimes have been a question with the Israelite which should die, the ass or the lamb? Would not the good man pause to estimate and compare? Assuredly there was no comparison between the value of the soul of man and the life of the Lord Jesus, and yet the Lamb dies, and man the ass is spared. My soul, admire the boundless love of God to thee and others of the human race. Worms are bought with the blood of the Son of the Highest! Dust and ashes redeemed with a price far above silver and gold! What a doom had been mine had not plenteous redemption been found! The breaking of the neck of the ass was but a momentary penalty, but who shall measure the wrath to come to which no limit can be imagined? Inestimably dear is the glorious Lamb who has redeemed us from such a doom.
