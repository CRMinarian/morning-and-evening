## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

When the good advice, the kind friends and even the Sunday services stop helping, could God be drawing you back to the fountain?

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Psalm thirty-six, verse nine. "For with thee is the fountain of life: in thy light shall we see light."

[beat]

With thee is the fountain of life.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** There are times in our spiritual lives when human advice and sympathy, and even the outward forms of religion, fail to comfort or help us. [beat] Why does our gracious God allow this? Perhaps because we have been living too much without him. So he takes away everything we have got used to leaning on, to drive us to himself.

**(2)** It is a blessed thing to live at the fountain head. While our water bottles are full, we are happy, like Hagar and Ishmael, to head off into the wilderness. But when they run dry, nothing will do for us except "Thou God seest me." We are like the prodigal son: we love the pig troughs, and forget our Father's house.

**(3)** And remember, we can make pig troughs and husks even out of the forms of religion. They are blessed things, but we can put them in God's place, and then they are worthless. Anything becomes an idol when it keeps us away from God. Even the bronze serpent was to be despised as "Nehushtan", a mere piece of brass, once people worshipped it instead of God.

**(4)** The prodigal was never safer than when he was driven to his father's arms, because he could find food nowhere else. [pause] Our Lord does us the kindness of a famine in the land, so that we will look for him all the more.

**(5)** The best place for a Christian is living wholly and directly on God's grace, still standing where he stood at the very beginning: "Having nothing, and yet possessing all things." Let us never for a moment think that our standing rests on our holiness, our self-denial, our virtues, or our feelings. No. Because Christ made a full atonement, we are saved; for we are complete in him.

**(6)** [slower] We have nothing of our own to trust in. We rest on the merits of Jesus. His suffering and his holy life give us the only sure ground of confidence. [beat] Beloved, when we are brought to the point of thirst, we are sure to turn eagerly to the fountain of life.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Father, forgive us for leaning on everything but you, even on good things given to us by you. When the bottles run dry, thank you that you are the fountain of life. Tonight we let go of our own holiness and feelings as grounds of hope, and rest on Christ's full atonement alone. Make us thirsty for you, and satisfy us from your fountain, through Jesus our Lord. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Drink from the fountain head. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 16**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Psalm 36:9**
>
> "With thee is the fountain of life."
>
> There are times in our spiritual experience when human counsel or sympathy, or religious ordinances, fail to comfort or help us. Why does our gracious God permit this? Perhaps it is because we have been living too much without him, and he therefore takes away everything upon which we have been in the habit of depending, that he may drive us to himself. It is a blessed thing to live at the fountain head. While our skin- bottles are full, we are content, like Hagar and Ishmael, to go into the wilderness; but when those are dry, nothing will serve us but "Thou God seest me." We are like the prodigal, we love the swine-troughs and forget our Father's house. Remember, we can make swine-troughs and husks even out of the forms of religion; they are blessed things, but we may put them in God's place, and then they are of no value. Anything becomes an idol when it keeps us away from God: even the brazen serpent is to be despised as "Nehushtan," if we worship it instead of God. The prodigal was never safer than when he was driven to his father's bosom, because he could find sustenance nowhere else. Our Lord favours us with a famine in the land that it may make us seek after himself the more. The best position for a Christian is living wholly and directly on God's grace--still abiding where he stood at first--"Having nothing, and yet possessing all things." Let us never for a moment think that our standing is in our sanctification, our mortification, our graces, or our feelings, but know that because Christ offered a full atonement, therefore we are saved; for we are complete in him. Having nothing of our own to trust to, but resting upon the merits of Jesus--his passion and holy life furnish us with the only sure ground of confidence. Beloved, when we are brought to a thirsting condition, we are sure to turn to the fountain of life with eagerness.
