## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Watch a parent on a packed Tube platform scoop up the smallest child first, and you have tonight's picture of the Good Shepherd.

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Isaiah, chapter forty, verse eleven. "He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young."

[beat]

He shall gather the lambs with his arm.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Our good Shepherd has all sorts in his flock. Some are strong in the Lord; others are weak in faith. But he cares for all his sheep without favourites, and the weakest lamb is as dear to him as the most advanced of the flock.

**(2)** Lambs tend to lag behind, they are inclined to wander, and they tire easily. But the Shepherd protects them from every danger of these weaknesses with his strong arm. He finds newborn souls, like young lambs, close to dying, and he feeds them until their life grows strong. He finds weak minds ready to faint, and he comforts them and renews their strength. [beat] He gathers all the little ones, because it is not the will of our heavenly Father that one of them should perish.

**(3)** [building] What a quick eye he must have, to see them all! What a tender heart, to care for them all! What a long and powerful arm, to gather them all! In his lifetime on earth, he was a great gatherer of the weaker ones. And now that he lives in heaven, his loving heart still goes out to the gentle and the broken, the timid and the frail, the fearful and the fainting, down here.

**(4)** [softer] How gently he gathered me to himself: to his truth, to his blood, to his love, to his church! With what effective grace he drew me to come to him! And since I first came, how often he has brought me back from my wanderings, and folded me once more inside the circle of his everlasting arm.

**(5)** The best of all is that he does it all himself, personally. He does not hand the task of love to someone else; he comes down himself to rescue and keep his most unworthy servant. [pause] How can I love him enough, or serve him as he deserves? I would gladly make his name great to the ends of the earth, but what can my weakness do for him?

**(6)** [slower] Great Shepherd, add to all your mercies this one more: a heart that loves you more truly, as I ought.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Good Shepherd, thank you that the weakest lamb is as dear to you as the strongest sheep. Thank you for gathering us, for bringing us back when we wander, and for doing it yourself. Tonight, carry the tired and the fainting, the timid and the frail, close to your heart. Give us hearts that love you more truly, and use even our weakness to make your name great. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Rest in the Shepherd's arm. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 17**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Isaiah 40:11**
>
> "He shall gather the lambs with his arm."
>
> Our good Shepherd has in his flock a variety of experiences, some are strong in the Lord, and others are weak in faith, but he is impartial in his care for all his sheep, and the weakest lamb is as dear to him as the most advanced of the flock. Lambs are wont to lag behind, prone to wander, and apt to grow weary, but from all the danger of these infirmities the Shepherd protects them with his arm of power. He finds new-born souls, like young lambs, ready to perish--he nourishes them till life becomes vigorous; he finds weak minds ready to faint and die--he consoles them and renews their strength. All the little ones he gathers, for it is not the will of our heavenly Father that one of them should perish. What a quick eye he must have to see them all! What a tender heart to care for them all! What a far- reaching and potent arm, to gather them all! In his lifetime on earth he was a great gatherer of the weaker sort, and now that he dwells in heaven, his loving heart yearns towards the meek and contrite, the timid and feeble, the fearful and fainting here below. How gently did he gather me to himself, to his truth, to his blood, to his love, to his church! With what effectual grace did he compel me to come to himself! Since my first conversion, how frequently has he restored me from my wanderings, and once again folded me within the circle of his everlasting arm! The best of all is, that he does it all himself personally, not delegating the task of love, but condescending himself to rescue and preserve his most unworthy servant. How shall I love him enough or serve him worthily? I would fain make his name great unto the ends of the earth, but what can my feebleness do for him? Great Shepherd, add to thy mercies this one other, a heart to love thee more truly as I ought.
