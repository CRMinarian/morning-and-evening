## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

It is perfectly possible to give generously, sing beautifully and serve on every rota at church, while quietly ignoring what Jesus actually said.

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

First Samuel, chapter fifteen, verse twenty-two. "And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams."

[beat]

Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Saul had been commanded to destroy the Amalekites completely, and their cattle. Instead, he spared the king, and let his people take the best of the oxen and the sheep. When he was called to account, he said he had done it in order to offer sacrifice to God. [beat] But Samuel met him at once with the plain truth: sacrifices are no excuse for an act of direct rebellion.

**(2)** This sentence deserves to be printed in gold letters and hung up in front of a generation that worships the wrong things: people who are very fond of the finery of religion they invent for themselves, but completely ignore the laws of God.

**(3)** Keep this always in mind. To keep strictly to the path of your Saviour's command is better than any outward form of religion. And to listen to his instruction with an attentive ear is better than bringing the fat of rams, or any other precious thing, to lay on his altar.

**(4)** [slower] If you are failing to keep even the least of Christ's commands to his disciples, I beg you, be disobedient no longer. [beat] All your claims of devotion to your Master, and all the devout things you may do, are no compensation for disobedience. "To obey," even in the slightest and smallest thing, "is better than sacrifice," however grand.

**(5)** [building] Do not talk about Gregorian chant, expensive robes, incense, and banners. The first thing God asks of his child is obedience. Though you should give your body to be burned, and all your goods to feed the poor, if you do not listen to the Lord's commands, all your ceremony will profit you nothing.

**(6)** It is a blessed thing to be teachable, like a little child. But it is a far more blessed thing, once you have been taught the lesson, to carry it out to the letter. [pause] How many decorate their churches and dress up their priests, but refuse to obey the word of the Lord! [softer] My soul, do not join them in their secret.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord Jesus, we confess that we would often rather offer you something impressive than simply do what you say. Forgive our disobedience, in the large things and the smallest. Give us attentive ears and willing hearts, so that we not only learn your lessons but carry them out to the letter. Let our worship be obedience first, tonight and tomorrow, for your name's sake. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Obey him, even in the small things. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 18**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: This reading contains no dashes, so nothing was altered.

> **1 Samuel 15:22**
>
> "Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice."
>
> Saul had been commanded to slay utterly all the Amalekites and their cattle. Instead of doing so, he preserved the king, and suffered his people to take the best of the oxen and of the sheep. When called to account for this, he declared that he did it with a view of offering sacrifice to God; but Samuel met him at once with the assurance that sacrifices were no excuse for an act of direct rebellion. The sentence before us is worthy to be printed in letters of gold, and to be hung up before the eyes of the present idolatrous generation, who are very fond of the fineries of will-worship, but utterly neglect the laws of God. Be it ever in your remembrance, that to keep strictly in the path of your Saviour's command is better than any outward form of religion; and to hearken to his precept with an attentive ear is better than to bring the fat of rams, or any other precious thing to lay upon his altar. If you are failing to keep the least of Christ's commands to his disciples, I pray you be disobedient no longer. All the pretensions you make of attachment to your Master, and all the devout actions which you may perform, are no recompense for disobedience. "To obey," even in the slightest and smallest thing, "is better than sacrifice," however pompous. Talk not of Gregorian chants, sumptuous robes, incense, and banners; the first thing which God requires of his child is obedience; and though you should give your body to be burned, and all your goods to feed the poor, yet if you do not hearken to the Lord's precepts, all your formalities shall profit you nothing. It is a blessed thing to be teachable as a little child, but it is a much more blessed thing when one has been taught the lesson, to carry it out to the letter. How many adorn their temples and decorate their priests, but refuse to obey the word of the Lord! My soul, come not thou into their secret.
