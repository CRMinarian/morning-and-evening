## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Anyone can sing when the sun is out and the bills are paid; the real question is who gives you a song in the dark.

[beat]

Good evening. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Job, chapter thirty-five, verse ten. "But none saith, Where is God my maker, who giveth songs in the night."

[beat]

God, my maker, who giveth songs in the night.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Anyone can sing in the daytime. [beat] When the cup is full, people find inspiration in it. When money is rolling in, anyone can praise the God who gives a good harvest, or brings a loaded ship safely home.

**(2)** It is easy enough for a wind chime to make music when the wind blows; the hard thing is for music to rise when there is not a breath of wind. It is easy to sing when we can read the notes by daylight. But the truly skilful singer is the one who sings when there is not a single ray of light to read by, who sings from the heart.

**(3)** No one can make a song in the night by themselves. You may try, but you will find that a song in the night has to be given by God. [pause] Let everything go well, and I can weave songs wherever I go, out of the flowers along my path. But put me in a desert, where nothing green grows, and how will I make a hymn of praise to God? How can a mortal make a crown for the Lord where there are no jewels?

**(4)** [building] Let this voice be clear and this body healthy, and I can sing God's praise. But silence my tongue, lay me on a hospital bed, and how will I sing God's high praises then, unless he himself gives me the song? No. It is not in our power to sing when everything is against us, unless a coal from God's altar touches our lips.

**(5)** [slower] It was a song from God that Habakkuk sang, when in the night he said:

[slower, almost spoken as verse]
"Although the fig tree shall not blossom,
neither shall fruit be in the vines;
the labour of the olive shall fail,
and the fields shall yield no meat;
the flock shall be cut off from the fold,
and there shall be no herd in the stalls:
yet I will rejoice in the Lord,
I will joy in the God of my salvation."

**(7)** [softer] So, since our Maker gives songs in the night, let us wait on him for the music. [pause] O chief musician, do not let us stay songless because trouble has come; tune our lips to the melody of thanksgiving.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

God our Maker, it is easy to praise you when the sun is out, and hard when the night comes. Some of us are in that night now. We cannot make the song ourselves. Give us songs in the dark, as you gave Habakkuk, so that even when the fields are bare we rejoice in you. Touch our lips, and tune them to thanksgiving, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

May he give you a song tonight. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Sleep well.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Evening, October 19**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Job 35:10**
>
> "God, my maker, who giveth songs in the night."
>
> Any man can sing in the day. When the cup is full, man draws inspiration from it. When wealth rolls in abundance around him, any man can praise the God who gives a plenteous harvest or sends home a loaded argosy. It is easy enough for an Aeolian harp to whisper music when the winds blow--the difficulty is for music to swell forth when no wind is stirring. It is easy to sing when we can read the notes by daylight; but he is skilful who sings when there is not a ray of light to read by--who sings from his heart. No man can make a song in the night of himself; he may attempt it, but he will find that a song in the night must be divinely inspired. Let all things go well, I can weave songs, fashioning them wherever I go out of the flowers that grow upon my path; but put me in a desert, where no green thing grows, and wherewith shall I frame a hymn of praise to God? How shall a mortal man make a crown for the Lord where no jewels are? Let but this voice be clear, and this body full of health, and I can sing God's praise: silence my tongue, lay me upon the bed of languishing, and how shall I then chant God's high praises, unless he himself give me the song? No, it is not in man's power to sing when all is adverse, unless an altar-coal shall touch his lip. It was a divine song, which Habakkuk sang, when in the night he said, "Although the fig-tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation." Then, since our Maker gives songs in the night, let us wait upon him for the music. O thou chief musician, let us not remain songless because affliction is upon us, but tune thou our lips to the melody of thanksgiving.
