## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Saying sorry is easy enough over a flat white, but what does it take to actually hate the thing you are sorry for?

[beat]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Second Corinthians, chapter seven, verse ten. "For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death."

[beat]

Godly sorrow worketh repentance.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Real, spiritual sorrow for sin is the work of the Spirit of God. [beat] Repentance is too rare a flower to grow in nature's garden. Pearls grow naturally in oysters, but repentance never shows itself in sinners unless divine grace works it in them. If you have one particle of real hatred for sin, God must have given it to you, for the thorns of human nature never produced a single fig. "That which is born of the flesh is flesh."

**(2)** Here is the second thing. True repentance looks to the Saviour. When we repent of sin, we must keep one eye on the sin and the other on the cross. Better still, fix both eyes on Christ, and see your sins only in the light of his love.

**(3)** And true sorrow for sin is practical. No one may say they hate sin while they go on living in it. [beat] Repentance makes us see the evil of sin, not as a theory, but from experience, the way a child who has been burnt dreads the fire. We will be as afraid of sin as someone who was mugged last week on the way home from the station is afraid of that dark stretch of road. And we will avoid it, avoid it in everything, not only in the big things but in the little ones, the way you would step back from a small adder as quickly as from a large snake.

**(4)** [building] True sorrow for sin will make us guard our tongue, in case it says a wrong word. It will make us watch our daily actions, in case we offend in anything. Each night we will close the day with honest confession of where we fell short, and each morning we will wake with earnest prayer that God would hold us up today, so that we do not sin against him.

**(5)** [slower] And real repentance is continual. Believers repent until their dying day. [pause] This is a well that never stops dripping. Every other sorrow fades with time, but this dear sorrow grows as we grow. It is such a sweet bitterness that we thank God we are allowed to taste it, and to bear it, until we enter our eternal rest.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Holy Spirit, we cannot grow repentance in our own hearts; you must plant it there. Give us a true hatred of sin, in the great things and the small. Turn both our eyes to Christ, so that we see our sin in the light of his love and his cross. Guard our tongues and our hands today, and hold us up so that we do not sin against you. Keep this sweet sorrow growing in us until we reach our rest, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Keep both eyes on Christ. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Go well today.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 13**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **2 Corinthians 7:10**
>
> "Godly sorrow worketh repentance."
>
> Genuine, spiritual mourning for sin is the work of the Spirit of God. Repentance is too choice a flower to grow in nature's garden. Pearls grow naturally in oysters, but penitence never shows itself in sinners except divine grace works it in them. If thou hast one particle of real hatred for sin, God must have given it thee, for human nature's thorns never produced a single fig. "That which is born of the flesh is flesh."
>
> True repentance has a distinct reference to the Saviour. When we repent of sin, we must have one eye upon sin and another upon the cross, or it will be better still if we fix both our eyes upon Christ and see our transgressions only, in the light of his love.
>
> True sorrow for sin is eminently practical. No man may say he hates sin, if he lives in it. Repentance makes us see the evil of sin, not merely as a theory, but experimentally--as a burnt child dreads fire. We shall be as much afraid of it, as a man who has lately been stopped and robbed is afraid of the thief upon the highway; and we shall shun it--shun it in everything--not in great things only, but in little things, as men shun little vipers as well as great snakes. True mourning for sin will make us very jealous over our tongue, lest it should say a wrong word; we shall be very watchful over our daily actions, lest in anything we offend, and each night we shall close the day with painful confessions of shortcoming, and each morning awaken with anxious prayers, that this day God would hold us up that we may not sin against him.
>
> Sincere repentance is continual. Believers repent until their dying day. This dropping well is not intermittent. Every other sorrow yields to time, but this dear sorrow grows with our growth, and it is so sweet a bitter, that we thank God we are permitted to enjoy and to suffer it until we enter our eternal rest.
