## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

You can read everything ever written about a person and still never have sat across a table from them, so do you actually know Jesus?

[beat]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Philippians, chapter three, verse eight. "Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ."

[beat]

I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Knowing Christ, really knowing him, is personal. [beat] I cannot know Jesus through someone else's friendship with him. No, I must know him myself. I must know him for myself.

**(2)** It is an informed knowledge too. I must know him, not as some dreamer imagines him, but as the Word reveals him. I must know his two natures, divine and human. I must know his offices, his character, his works, his shame, and his glory. I must think about him until I "comprehend with all saints what is the breadth, and length, and depth, and height; and know the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge."

**(3)** It is a loving knowledge. If I know him at all, I must love him. [pause] An ounce of heart knowledge is worth a ton of head learning.

**(4)** It is a satisfying knowledge. When I know my Saviour, my mind is full to the brim; I have what my spirit was longing for. "This is that bread whereof if a man eat he shall never hunger."

**(5)** And yet, at the same time, it is an exciting knowledge. The more I know of my Beloved, the more I want to know. The higher I climb, the higher the peaks that call me on. I want more the more I get. Like a miser with his money, my gold makes me hungry for more.

**(6)** [building] And to finish: this knowledge of Christ Jesus is a deeply happy one. It lifts me so high that sometimes it carries me clean above every trial, every doubt, every sorrow. While I enjoy it, it makes me something more than "Man that is born of woman, who is of few days, and full of trouble"; for it wraps me in the deathless life of the ever living Saviour, and ties around me the golden belt of his eternal joy.

**(7)** [slower] Come, my soul. Sit at Jesus's feet, and learn all this from him, all day today.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord Jesus, we do not want to know you second hand. Show yourself to us in your Word, in your shame and in your glory, until our hearts love what our minds learn. Fill us today, and keep us hungry for more of you. When trials and doubts come, lift us above them with the joy of knowing you. Teach us at your feet this morning, and keep us there all day. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Sit at his feet all day. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Go well today.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 14**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Philippians 3:8**
>
> "I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord."
>
> Spiritual knowledge of Christ will be a personal knowledge. I cannot know Jesus through another person's acquaintance with him. No, I must know him myself; I must know him on my own account. It will be an intelligent knowledge--I must know him, not as the visionary dreams of him, but as the Word reveals him. I must know his natures, divine and human. I must know his offices--his attributes--his works--his shame--his glory. I must meditate upon him until I "comprehend with all saints what is the breadth, and length, and depth, and height; and know the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge." It will be an affectionate knowledge of him; indeed, if I know him at all, I must love him. An ounce of heart knowledge is worth a ton of head learning. Our knowledge of him will be a satisfying knowledge. When I know my Saviour, my mind will be full to the brim--I shall feel that I have that which my spirit panted after. "This is that bread whereof if a man eat he shall never hunger." At the same time it will be an exciting knowledge; the more I know of my Beloved, the more I shall want to know. The higher I climb the loftier will be the summits which invite my eager footsteps. I shall want the more as I get the more. Like the miser's treasure, my gold will make me covet more. To conclude; this knowledge of Christ Jesus will be a most happy one; in fact, so elevating, that sometimes it will completely bear me up above all trials, and doubts, and sorrows; and it will, while I enjoy it, make me something more than "Man that is born of woman, who is of few days, and full of trouble"; for it will fling about me the immortality of the ever living Saviour, and gird me with the golden girdle of his eternal joy. Come, my soul, sit at Jesus's feet and learn of him all this day.
