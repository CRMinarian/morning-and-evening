## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Suppose Christ walked into this coffee shop now, not as a baby in a manger but as Judge: could you look him in the eye?

[beat]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Malachi, chapter three, verse two. "But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner's fire, and like fullers' soap."

[beat]

But who may abide the day of his coming?.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** When Christ came the first time, he came without outward show or display of power. And yet, in truth, very few could stand up to his testing. [beat] Herod was shaken, and all Jerusalem with him, at the news of that wonderful birth. Those who thought they were waiting for him exposed their own claims as hollow by rejecting him when he arrived. His life on earth was a winnowing fan, sifting the great heap of religious profession, and few enough came through it.

**(2)** But what will his second coming be like? What sinner can bear to think of it? "He shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked."

**(3)** [building] When he was humbled, he only said to the soldiers, "I am he," and they fell backwards. What will the terror of his enemies be when he reveals himself fully as the great "I am"? His death shook the earth and darkened the sky. What will be the dreadful splendour of that day, when he comes as the living Saviour and summons the living and the dead before him?

**(4)** [pause] If only the terrors of the Lord would persuade people to leave their sins and kiss the Son, before he is angry! Though he is a lamb, he is still the lion of the tribe of Judah, tearing the prey to pieces. He will not break the bruised reed; and yet he will break his enemies with a rod of iron, and smash them like a potter's jar. None of his enemies will stand up before the storm of his wrath, or hide from the driving hail of his anger.

**(5)** [softer] But his beloved people, washed in his blood, look for his appearing with joy, and they hope to meet it without fear. For them he sits as a refiner even now, and when he has tested them, they will come out as gold.

**(6)** [slower] So let us search ourselves this morning, and make our calling and election sure, so that the coming of the Lord brings no dark dread to our minds. [beat] Oh, for grace to throw away all hypocrisy, and to be found by him sincere and without blame on the day he appears.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord Jesus, you came once in humility, and you will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead. We confess that we could never stand before you in ourselves. Wash us in your blood, refine us like gold, and strip away every pretence in us. Search our hearts this morning, make our calling and election sure, and let us look for your appearing with joy and not with fear. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Live ready for his appearing. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Go well today.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 15**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: This reading contains no dashes, so nothing was altered.

> **Malachi 3:2**
>
> "But who may abide the day of his coming?"
>
> His first coming was without external pomp or show of power, and yet in truth there were few who could abide its testing might. Herod and all Jerusalem with him were stirred at the news of the wondrous birth. Those who supposed themselves to be waiting for him, showed the fallacy of their professions by rejecting him when he came. His life on earth was a winnowing fan, which tried the great heap of religious profession, and few enough could abide the process. But what will his second advent be? What sinner can endure to think of it? "He shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked." When in his humiliation he did but say to the soldiers, "I am he," they fell backward; what will be the terror of his enemies when he shall more fully reveal himself as the "I am?" His death shook earth and darkened heaven, what shall be the dreadful splendour of that day in which as the living Saviour, he shall summon the quick and dead before him? O that the terrors of the Lord would persuade men to forsake their sins and kiss the Son lest he be angry! Though a lamb, he is yet the lion of the tribe of Judah, rending the prey in pieces; and though he breaks not the bruised reed, yet will he break his enemies with a rod of iron, and dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel. None of his foes shall bear up before the tempest of his wrath, or hide themselves from the sweeping hail of his indignation; but his beloved blood washed people look for his appearing with joy, and hope to abide it without fear: to them he sits as a refiner even now, and when he has tried them they shall come forth as gold. Let us search ourselves this morning and make our calling and election sure, so that the coming of the Lord may cause no dark forebodings in our mind. O for grace to cast away all hypocrisy, and to be found of him sincere and without rebuke in the day of his appearing.
