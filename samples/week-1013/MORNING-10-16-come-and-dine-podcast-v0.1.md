## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Picture a fire on a beach at dawn, breakfast already cooking, and the risen Jesus calling tired fishermen over to eat.

[beat]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

John, chapter twenty-one, verse twelve. "Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine. And none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou? knowing that it was the Lord."

[beat]

Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** In these words the believer is invited into a holy nearness to Jesus. "Come and dine" means the same table and the same food. Yes, and sometimes it means sitting side by side, and leaning our head on the Saviour's chest. It is being brought into the banqueting house, where the banner over us is redeeming love.

**(2)** "Come and dine" also gives us a picture of union with Jesus, because the only food we can feast on when we eat with Jesus is Jesus himself. [pause] Oh, what a union this is! It is a depth that reason cannot measure, that we feed on Jesus like this. "He that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, dwelleth in me, and I in him."

**(3)** It is also an invitation to enjoy fellowship with the saints. Christians may differ on all sorts of points, but they all have one spiritual appetite. [beat] We may not all feel alike, but we can all feed alike, on the bread of life sent down from heaven. At the table of fellowship with Jesus, we are one bread and one cup. Like friends passing round one shared cup, we pledge ourselves to one another, wholeheartedly.

**(4)** Get nearer to Jesus, and you will find yourself joined more and more in spirit to everyone who, like you, lives on the same heavenly manna. If we were nearer to Jesus, we would be nearer to one another.

**(5)** And in these words we also see where every Christian's strength comes from. To look at Christ is to live; but for strength to serve him, you must "come and dine". [beat] We struggle with a great deal of unnecessary weakness because we neglect this instruction from the Master. None of us needs to put ourselves on a starvation diet. On the contrary, we should feast on the richest food of the gospel, so that we build up strength, and stretch every power to the full in the Master's service.

**(6)** [slower] So then, if you want to know nearness to Jesus, union with Jesus, love for his people, and strength from Jesus, "come and dine" with him, by faith.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord Jesus, you call us to your table, and you yourself are the food. Draw us near this morning, close enough to lean on you. Feed us on yourself, so that we are one with you and one with all your people, however much we differ. Forgive us for living on so little, and strengthen us for your service today. We come and dine, by faith. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Come and dine, and be strong. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Go well today.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 16**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: This reading contains no dashes, so nothing was altered.

> **John 21:12**
>
> "Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine."
>
> In these words the believer is invited to a holy nearness to Jesus. "Come and dine," implies the same table, the same meat; aye, and sometimes it means to sit side by side, and lean our head upon the Saviour's bosom. It is being brought into the banqueting-house, where waves the banner of redeeming love. "Come and dine," gives us a vision of union with Jesus, because the only food that we can feast upon when we dine with Jesus is himself. Oh, what union is this! It is a depth which reason cannot fathom, that we thus feed upon Jesus. "He that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, dwelleth in me, and I in him." It is also an invitation to enjoy fellowship with the saints. Christians may differ on a variety of points, but they have all one spiritual appetite; and if we cannot all feel alike, we can all feed alike on the bread of life sent down from heaven. At the table of fellowship with Jesus we are one bread and one cup. As the loving cup goes round we pledge one another heartily therein. Get nearer to Jesus, and you will find yourself linked more and more in spirit to all who are like yourself, supported by the same heavenly manna. If we were more near to Jesus we should be more near to one another. We likewise see in these words the source of strength for every Christian. To look at Christ is to live, but for strength to serve him you must "come and dine." We labour under much unnecessary weakness on account of neglecting this precept of the Master. We none of us need to put ourselves on low diet; on the contrary, we should fatten on the marrow and fatness of the gospel that we may accumulate strength therein, and urge every power to its full tension in the Master's service. Thus, then, if you would realize nearness to Jesus, union with Jesus, love to his people and strength from Jesus, "come and dine" with him by faith.
