## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Flick back through the diary of your own life: is there one single page where God actually let you down?

[beat]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

First Samuel, chapter twenty-seven, verse one. "And David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul: there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape into the land of the Philistines."

[beat]

And David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** What David thought in his heart at this point was a false thought. [beat] He had no grounds at all for thinking that God's anointing him through Samuel was meant to be an empty, meaningless act. Not once had the Lord deserted his servant. David had often been put in dangerous positions, but there had not been a single case where God had not stepped in and rescued him.

**(2)** His trials had been varied. They had not come in one shape only, but in many. And yet in every case, the one who sent the trial had also kindly provided a way of escape. David could not put his finger on one entry in his diary and say, "Here is evidence that the Lord will abandon me," because the whole course of his life proved the very opposite. He should have reasoned from what God had done for him, that God would go on defending him.

**(3)** [building] But is that not exactly how we doubt God's help? Is it not mistrust without a cause? Have we ever had the shadow of a reason to doubt our Father's goodness? [beat] Have his kindnesses not been marvellous? Has he once failed to justify our trust? [pause] Oh, no. Our God has never left us, not once.

**(4)** We have had dark nights, but the star of his love has shone through the blackness. We have been in hard fights, but over our heads he has held up the shield of our defence. We have gone through many trials, never to our harm, always to our good. And the conclusion from our past experience is this: he who has been with us in six troubles will not abandon us in the seventh.

**(5)** What we have known of our faithful God proves that he will keep us to the end. [slower] So let us not reason against the evidence. How could we ever be so ungenerous as to doubt our God?

**(6)** [softer] Lord, throw down the Jezebel of our unbelief, and let the dogs devour it.

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Faithful Father, you have never once failed us, and still we doubt you. Forgive our mistrust without a cause. Remind us this morning of every dark night when your love shone through, and every fight where you held up our shield. Because you have kept us in six troubles, teach us to trust you in the seventh. Throw down our unbelief, and keep us to the end, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

He has never once failed you. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Go well today.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 17**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **1 Samuel 27:1**
>
> "And David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul."
>
> The thought of David's heart at this time was a false thought, because he certainly had no ground for thinking that God's anointing him by Samuel was intended to be left as an empty unmeaning act. On no one occasion had the Lord deserted his servant; he had been placed in perilous positions very often, but not one instance had occurred in which divine interposition had not delivered him. The trials to which he had been exposed had been varied; they had not assumed one form only, but many--yet in every case he who sent the trial had also graciously ordained a way of escape. David could not put his finger upon any entry in his diary, and say of it, "Here is evidence that the Lord will forsake me," for the entire tenor of his past life proved the very reverse. He should have argued from what God had done for him, that God would be his defender still. But is it not just in the same way that we doubt God's help? Is it not mistrust without a cause? Have we ever had the shadow of a reason to doubt our Father's goodness? Have not his lovingkindnesses been marvellous? Has he once failed to justify our trust? Ah, no! our God has not left us at any time. We have had dark nights, but the star of love has shone forth amid the blackness; we have been in stern conflicts, but over our head he has held aloft the shield of our defence. We have gone through many trials, but never to our detriment, always to our advantage; and the conclusion from our past experience is, that he who has been with us in six troubles, will not forsake us in the seventh. What we have known of our faithful God, proves that he will keep us to the end. Let us not, then, reason contrary to evidence. How can we ever be so ungenerous as to doubt our God? Lord, throw down the Jezebel of our unbelief, and let the dogs devour it.
