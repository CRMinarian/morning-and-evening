## Script

[music: theme sting, contemporary coffee-shop acoustic, then under]

Why do some of us feel so spiritually thin, when the path of prayer is open to the weakest believer in London?

[beat]

Good morning. This is Spurgeon Unbound.

[music out] [pause]

### The verse

Psalm sixty-five, verse eleven. "Thou crownest the year with thy goodness; and thy paths drop fatness."

[beat]

Thy paths drop fatness.

[pause]

### Meditation

**(1)** Many are the paths of the Lord that "drop fatness", that drip with richness. But one especially is the path of prayer. [beat] No believer who spends much time alone with God will need to cry, "My leanness, my leanness; woe unto me." Starving souls live a long way from the mercy-seat, and they become like parched fields in a drought.

**(2)** Winning with God in wrestling prayer is sure to make a believer strong, if not always happy. The nearest place to the gate of heaven is the throne of grace. [pause] Much alone with Jesus, and you will have much assurance. Little alone with Jesus, and your faith will be shallow, clouded with doubts and fears, and not sparkling with the joy of the Lord.

**(3)** [building] The soul-enriching path of prayer is open to the very weakest saint. No high attainments are required. You are not told to come because you are an advanced saint; you are freely invited if you are a saint at all. So see to it, friend, that you are often in the place of private prayer. Be much on your knees, for that is how Elijah brought the rain down on Israel's starving fields.

**(4)** There is another special path that drips with richness for those who walk it: the secret walk of communion. Oh, the delight of fellowship with Jesus! [beat] There are no words on earth for the holy calm of a soul leaning on Jesus.

**(5)** Few Christians understand it. They live in the lowlands, and seldom climb to the top of Mount Nebo. They live in the outer court; they never go into the holy place; they do not take up their privilege as priests. They see the sacrifice from a distance, but they never sit down with the priest to eat of it, and enjoy the richness of the offering.

**(6)** [softer] But you, friend, sit always in the shade of Jesus. Come up to that palm tree, and take hold of its branches. Let your Beloved be to you like the apple tree among the trees of the wood, and you will be satisfied as with the richest food. [pause] O Jesus, visit us with your salvation!

[long pause]

### Prayer

[softer]

Lord Jesus, forgive us for living so far from the mercy-seat, and for wondering why our souls feel thin. Draw us to our knees today, the weakest of us included. Bring us out of the lowlands and the outer court, into the holy calm of fellowship with you. Let us sit in your shade and be satisfied. Visit us with your salvation this morning. Amen.

[pause]

### Outro

[music: theme, under]

Be much alone with Jesus. This has been Spurgeon Unbound. Go well today.

[music: theme, up and out]

## Appendix: Original (public domain)

C. H. Spurgeon, *Morning and Evening: Daily Readings*, **Morning, October 18**, as given in the seed corpus (CCEL edition). Dash check: The original uses CCEL double hyphens; they are kept verbatim in this appendix only, and none appear in the script.

> **Psalm 65:11**
>
> "Thy paths drop fatness."
>
> Many are "the paths of the Lord" which "drop fatness," but an especial one is the path of prayer. No believer, who is much in the closet, will have need to cry, "My leanness, my leanness; woe unto me." Starving souls live at a distance from the mercy- seat, and become like the parched fields in times of drought. Prevalence with God in wrestling prayer is sure to make the believer strong--if not happy. The nearest place to the gate of heaven is the throne of the heavenly grace. Much alone, and you will have much assurance; little alone with Jesus, your religion will be shallow, polluted with many doubts and fears, and not sparkling with the joy of the Lord. Since the soul-enriching path of prayer is open to the very weakest saint; since no high attainments are required; since you are not bidden to come because you are an advanced saint, but freely invited if you be a saint at all; see to it, dear reader, that you are often in the way of private devotion. Be much on your knees, for so Elijah drew the rain upon famished Israel's fields.
>
> There is another especial path dropping with fatness to those who walk therein, it is the secret walk of communion. Oh! the delights of fellowship with Jesus! Earth hath no words which can set forth the holy calm of a soul leaning on Jesus' bosom. Few Christians understand it, they live in the lowlands and seldom climb to the top of Nebo: they live in the outer court, they enter not the holy place, they take not up the privilege of priesthood. At a distance they see the sacrifice, but they sit not down with the priest to eat thereof, and to enjoy the fat of the burnt offering. But, reader, sit thou ever under the shadow of Jesus; come up to that palm tree, and take hold of the branches thereof; let thy beloved be unto thee as the apple-tree among the trees of the wood, and thou shalt be satisfied as with marrow and fatness. O Jesus, visit us with thy salvation!
